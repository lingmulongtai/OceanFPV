# Metre-scale coastal ground detail

The 90 m Rocky Terrain photograph originally supplied the dominant island colour and normal. Its large blocks implied rock faces absent from the terrain geometry. Procedural islands now use the existing 4K [Seaside Rock by Dimitrios Savva](https://polyhaven.com/a/seaside_rock), at its published 2 m width, for primary rock colour and normals. The lighthouse reef and islands share the same three loaded texture objects. No additional asset files or image allocations are introduced.

Three world-space projections retain the existing physical scale on steep slopes. The 90 m colour supplies up to 35% relative variation: divide its sample by the coarsest mip's mean colour, then blend that ratio with one before multiplying the 2 m colour. Its strength is multiplied by `smoothstep(0.45,0.75,abs(normalize(worldNormal).y))`: slopes below approximately 41 degrees keep the full variation, while slopes above approximately 63 degrees use fine colour alone. This removes unsupported broad slab boundaries on exposed steep faces without suppressing the lower ground's colour variation. The normal is the interpolated geometric normal, before texture perturbation, so individual normal-map grains do not flicker between material strengths. Explicit LOD 16 clamps to the existing 2K texture's 1 x 1 mip; a 0.04 denominator floor bounds very dark channels. This avoids multiplying two full diffuse photographs. Fine roughness contributes 65%; fine normals replace the unsupported broad photographic relief before blending with grass. Full grass cover retains the existing 15 m material. Wet-shore shading runs after the completed blend. The layer changes shading; vertices, contacts and placements are untouched.

The optional layer requires all three detail maps. One missing map leaves macro rock and grass active. Stable uniform bindings also preserve restored cached shader variants when maps disappear and return. Normal/roughness/diffuse maps are loaded as one existing coherent group, so partial loads are disposed together.

## Evidence and limits

- The production material helper passes its original 96 GPU cases plus 1,728 detail cases. These cover signed and oblique planes, flat/tilted normals, patterned 2 m maps, colour/normal/roughness outputs, grass blending, wetness, and complete/incomplete/missing/restored states on the same material. Known coarse mip values differ from base pixels to exercise macro normalization. Analytical planar gradients provide independent expected normals. Maximum error: native RTX 4050 0.00006317; Vulkan SwiftShader 0.00004846.
- Actual scene, single-map failure and GPU projection checks pass on the native GPU (3/3, 1.3 minutes). The linked ground program uses 10 active samplers, within the checked device's 16-unit limit. Each shared detail image is requested once. The full 113 unit tests and strict production build passed for that composition release. Its subsequent 26-test PR/main suites and publication checks also passed; the separate steep-face follow-up still requires complete CI and publication verification.
- Eight fixed-camera baseline/adjustment comparisons and six final production captures use the actual main renderer, RTX 4050, ULTRA 1920 x 1280 buffer, and sea/vessel time 120. Day/evening views include the northern face, wider island and a close drainage face. All capture runs report no browser errors. Large slab cues are weaker and fine grain remains, but the material is darker and the underlying smooth heightfield is more apparent.

The broad photographed pattern still repeats on gentler slopes. A comparison that globally blurred macro colour and removed macro normals produced uniformly brown, smooth slopes and was rejected. The slope-dependent adjustment is limited to macro colour; it retains fine detail on steep faces and the full existing variation on gentler ones. This layer does not solve repetition, add rock silhouette geometry, or establish physical-controller acceptance, sustained frame rate, or parity on every GPU/browser.

## Complete loading and bounded recovery

Linux CI recorded a Seaside Rock roughness response failing during body retrieval with `TimeoutError`: total elapsed 25,000 ms, body elapsed 10,736 ms, and an aborted deadline signal. Its diffuse and normal images had completed. The loader previously discarded that entire material group and kept the standard surface.

A confirmed deadline during fetch or body retrieval now retries only that image once, with the same 25-second acquisition deadline for each attempt. Completed peer images stay loaded. Decode errors and unrelated failures do not retry. A second failure retains coherent group fallback and disposes any completed textures. Diagnostic records retain phase, error, elapsed time, attempt and recovery status; complete-scene checks require all three maps and no unresolved failure or fallback note.

The production loader records 15 controlled failures covering fetch/body/decode failures and two persistent deadline attempts per map, plus a recovery case that verifies diffuse/normal are each requested once and roughness twice. A native RTX 4050 full-scene regression deliberately aborts the actual 4K roughness response after HTTP headers: the next request restores all three maps and a normal startup note (normal/recovery scenes 2/2, 1.4 minutes). Existing native scene/fallback/GPU checks also passed 3/3, and the updated fixture passed under SwiftShader. The strict build and 113 unit tests passed for that release. The complete 26-test PR/main suites and deployment then succeeded. Public bundle `index-DfhG3r3P.js` matched its source SHA-256 on 2026-10-17; native BALANCED controls and ULTRA1920×1280 both loaded all materials without fallback or browser errors.

This recovers one expired request; persistent failures still use standard materials. It does not establish a universal network or startup-time guarantee.

## Preserve photographed albedo

The terrain's height-based green/brown vertex colours previously multiplied both the completed rock photograph and the grass blend. Captured coastal boulders used their original photographed albedo, making adjacent terrain appear artificially dark and green. After surface loading completes, ground materials with a diffuse photograph now disable vertex colours. Missing photographs retain the painted terrain fallback. The photographed cliff and captured boulders retain their existing materials; texture scale, normals, roughness, wetness, geometry and contacts are unchanged.

Eight comparisons used the same camera, light, sea/vessel time 120 and shader while switching island vertex colours between their original values and white. The northern headland and measured southwest islet were compared in day and golden light on RTX4050/D3D11, ULTRA1920×1280, effective FOV65. Bare rock returns to grey and the ground/boulder colour gap decreases; the smooth procedural northern shape and repeated boulders remain visible. Six captures of the actual implementation report no browser errors. Native browser checks pass for normal loading, one missing fine-detail map, and all photographs unavailable (3/3); they verify that painted fallback remains enabled only in the last case. Complete CI and public verification for this follow-up remain pending.
