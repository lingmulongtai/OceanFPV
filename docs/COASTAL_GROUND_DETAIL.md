# Metre-scale coastal ground detail

The 90 m Rocky Terrain photograph originally supplied the dominant island colour and normal. Its large blocks implied rock faces absent from the terrain geometry. Procedural islands now use the existing 4K [Seaside Rock by Dimitrios Savva](https://polyhaven.com/a/seaside_rock), at its published 2 m width, for primary rock colour and normals. The lighthouse reef and islands share the same three loaded texture objects. No additional asset files or image allocations are introduced.

Three world-space projections retain the existing physical scale on steep slopes. The 90 m colour remains as 35% relative variation: divide its sample by the coarsest mip's mean colour, then blend that ratio with one before multiplying the 2 m colour. Explicit LOD 16 clamps to the existing 2K texture's 1 x 1 mip; a 0.04 denominator floor bounds very dark channels. This avoids multiplying two full diffuse photographs. Fine roughness contributes 65%; fine normals replace the unsupported broad photographic relief before blending with grass. Full grass cover retains the existing 15 m material. Wet-shore shading runs after the completed blend. The layer changes shading; vertices, contacts and placements are untouched.

The optional layer requires all three detail maps. One missing map leaves macro rock and grass active. Stable uniform bindings also preserve restored cached shader variants when maps disappear and return. Normal/roughness/diffuse maps are loaded as one existing coherent group, so partial loads are disposed together.

## Evidence and limits

- The production material helper passes its original 96 GPU cases plus 1,728 detail cases. These cover signed and oblique planes, flat/tilted normals, patterned 2 m maps, colour/normal/roughness outputs, grass blending, wetness, and complete/incomplete/missing/restored states on the same material. Known coarse mip values differ from base pixels to exercise macro normalization. Analytical planar gradients provide independent expected normals. Maximum error: native RTX 4050 0.00006317; Vulkan SwiftShader 0.00004846.
- Actual scene, single-map failure and GPU projection checks pass on the native GPU (3/3, 1.3 minutes). The linked ground program uses 10 active samplers, within the checked device's 16-unit limit. Each shared detail image is requested once. The full 113 unit tests and strict production build pass. The complete 25-test browser suite passed before this composition adjustment; a complete suite on the adjusted composition remains pending.
- Eight fixed-camera baseline/adjustment comparisons and six final production captures use the actual main renderer, RTX 4050, ULTRA 1920 x 1280 buffer, and sea/vessel time 120. Day/evening views include the northern face, wider island and a close drainage face. All capture runs report no browser errors. Large slab cues are weaker and fine grain remains, but the material is darker and the underlying smooth heightfield is more apparent.

The broad photographed pattern still repeats at reduced contrast. A comparison that strongly blurred macro colour and removed macro normals produced uniformly brown, smooth slopes and was rejected. This layer does not solve repetition, add rock silhouette geometry, or establish physical-controller acceptance, sustained frame rate, or parity on every GPU/browser.
