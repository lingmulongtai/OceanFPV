# Ocean realism references

Reviewed 2026-10-13. These are technical precedents, not claims of matching their complete renderer.

- [Tessendorf, Simulating Ocean Water (author publications)](https://jtessen.people.clemson.edu/reports/index.html): Gaussian wave spectra, Hermitian time evolution, IFFT height fields and horizontal crest sharpening.
- [Ocean synthesis presentation, Tessendorf et al. 2022](https://jtessen.people.clemson.edu/gilligan/html/Os2022_tessendorf_reinhardt_gao.pdf): combining independent fields to reduce tiling repetition.
- [NVIDIA Triton Ocean SDK](https://developer.nvidia.com/triton-ocean-sdk): FFT sea, Kelvin wakes, foam/spray and wind-driven interaction as a complete visual system.
- [Epic Water Waves documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/simulating-waves-using-the-water-waves-asset): lateral motion sharpens wave crests; adjustable wavelength/amplitude alone does not guarantee natural detail.
- [NVIDIA GPU Gems, Effective Water Simulation from Physical Models](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models): surface derivatives and layered wave detail should remain consistent with geometric wave motion.
- [Epic Single Layer Water shading model](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/single-layer-water-shading-model?application_version=4.27): absorption and scattering depend on the water volume. This informs depth-based coastal colour; OceanFPV does not implement Epic's volume/refraction pass.
- [Poseidon browser ocean](https://github.com/owenyuwono/poseidon): independent wavelength cascades, Jacobian foam, Fresnel/absorption/scatter and matching sky illumination. Its WebGPU implementation is a reference, not copied into this WebGL2 renderer.
- [Bruneton, Neyret and Holzschuch, Real-time Realistic Ocean Lighting](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2009.01618.x): distribute wave detail between geometry, normals and the BRDF as its screen resolution changes.
- [Filament material guide, specular anti-aliasing](https://google.github.io/filament/main/materials.html#materialdefinitions/materialblock/anti-aliasing:specularantialiasing): use variance to suppress specular aliasing, with a bound to avoid excessive roughness.
- [MIT, The Art of Approximation in Science and Engineering](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-055j-the-art-of-approximation-in-science-and-engineering-spring-2008/311773ba1f658167d87a65f7cd05c045_book.pdf): the deep-water gravity/capillary dispersion relation combines gravity and surface-tension contributions to frequency.
- Original OceanLab MIT shaders: `spectrum_compute.comp`, `spectrum_modulate.comp`, `fft_unpack.comp`. The browser implementation uses conventional unshifted row/column transforms instead of the original transposed compute pipeline.

OceanFPV's spectral solver uses a Phillips wind spectrum with independent seeded Gaussian coefficients, three disjoint wavelength bands and noncommensurate 433/97/23.3 m patches. Its normalized CPU IFFT supplies the same displacement data to WebGL textures and collision/buoyancy sampling. This is a real spectral simulation, not a repeating photograph of water. The shading and foam/wakes remain real-time approximations; this is not CFD, path tracing or the Unreal renderer.

The marine follow-up combines unnormalized displacement tangents before deriving surface normals. A conservative bound on the cascades' summed symmetric compression limits lateral sharpening independently of wave height, avoiding excessive crest folding in SWELL while retaining its 2.15× height scale. Rendering, tangents and CPU inverse-displacement sampling share that horizontal scale. Whitecaps respond to compression and deformed-surface steepness; changing presets or rewinding time clears stale foam. The upward-facing normal limit remains, and persistent foam is still a per-cascade approximation. This bounds the sampled tangent fields; finite mesh triangles and central-difference derivatives remain approximations to continuous water.

Coastal depths use a packed 193×193 float texture of the three islands' actual terrain vertices. Explicit triangle interpolation matches the rendered terrain diagonal even without float linear filtering. The depth of the smaller lighthouse rock remains an ellipsoid approximation. This replaces detached circular shoreline rings with depth-dependent shallow water and wash at the visible coast.

The horizon follow-up uses pixel footprints to filter the unnormalized tangent fields before deriving a surface normal. The previous whole-band fade incorrectly removed every wavelength in a cascade when its shortest wave became unresolved: at18m/pixel, it removed the433m wave even though that period still covered24pixels. Each existing128×128 field now has CPU-averaged mip levels down to1×1. Explicit `LOD=clamp(log2(max(1,footprint*128/patchLength)),0,7)` retains resolvable longer waves in the same cascade. All levels keep the base texture's half-texel UV offset, so their averaged samples stay in phase. Native float filtering uses trilinear sampling; the fallback uses manual periodic bilinear sampling and interpolation between levels. Integer fallback levels take only four taps.

Squared bilinear fields are integrated over each periodic cell to measure retained slope and height energy. Adjacent-level cross-energy accounts for their shifted texel centres; fractional levels use `(1-f)^2*E0+2*f*(1-f)*C+f^2*E1`. Only energy missing from the original FFT fields contributes to isotropic GGX roughness. Coastal envelope gradients use height samples at the same mip levels as the tangents. The same final normal controls Fresnel and reflection distortion. Reflection mip levels account for the distorted screen footprint and unresolved slopes. Derivatives are evaluated before hull fragments are discarded. Geometry, whitecaps and CPU contacts explicitly retain the full-resolution field. The renderer still uses11 samplers; mip storage adds approximately0.75MiB to the nine float textures. This is a box-filtered tangent approximation with isotropic slope statistics, not the cited paper's complete hierarchical renderer, an anisotropic footprint filter or exact convolution of the deformed normal/BRDF.

The FFT follow-up caches size-specific bit-reversal swap pairs and Float64 stage twiddles, retaining positive inverse-transform phases and1/N² normalization. Height and X displacement now share one complex IFFT: `DxHat=i*directionX*HHat`, so `HHat+i*DxHat=(1-directionX)*HHat`; real and imaginary spatial outputs supply height and Dx respectively. Z displacement retains its separate Hermitian transform. Nyquist-axis direction zeros remain, and the number of128² IFFTs falls from9 to6 per spectral update without removing frequencies. The complex work buffers also save0.75MiB. Geometry, materials, wave strengths, field resolution and30Hz update cadence remain the same.

Validation: independent complex IDFT, Parseval, Hermitian/DC/Nyquist and mixed-size cache tests pass. An independent DFT of the public height field reconstructs both lateral displacements at size8/16 and time0/6/120. All83 unit tests, strict build and native coastal/mip GPU probe pass. Nine full-resolution snapshots across the three sea strengths and time0/6/120 compare5308416 Float32 displacement/tangent/foam values with the pre-change implementation; all sampled values are bit-identical, while scalar Float64 statistics differ by at most1.23e-15. This sampled comparison does not assert bit identity at every possible time.

A sequential development-machine Node benchmark uses40 warmups followed by100 complete spectral/foam updates at fixed0.04s increments. Its median/p95 before optimization were22.40/27.14ms, after cached plans19.85/29.63ms, and after packed transforms15.35/18.18ms. These are full sea-update timings, not FFT-only estimates or browser FPS guarantees. Native flight profiling, temporal appearance and complete browser checks remain separate validation scopes.

The covariance follow-up folds adjacent coarse-cell interpolation integrals into a separable kernel `[1,25,70,70,25,1]/192`. Horizontal then vertical six-tap filtering centres on fine coordinate `2*i+0.5`; the fine indices are `2*i-2` through `2*i+3` with periodic wrapping. The coarse field multiplies the filtered fine field, then averages over coarse texels. This is algebraically the same bilinear cross-energy integral, including1×1 final levels and repeated periodic taps. A reused Float64 scratch buffer prevents extra Float32 quantization and adds0.5625MiB across the nine fields.

Independent two-point Gauss quadrature splits each coarse cell at the fine interpolation knots and checks size2/4/8/16 cross-energy within1e-12. All84 unit tests, strict build and native coastal/mip GPU probe pass. A frozen time6/BREEZE comparison keeps energy exactly equal and cross-energy within2.56e-15 of the previous method. A Node benchmark of the nine complete mip/statistics updates (40 warmups,100 samples) measured median/p95 changing from7.31/8.69ms to4.53/6.07ms. Field data, interpolation, BRDF model and geometric/contact sampling are unchanged; this timing is a CPU diagnostic, not a browser FPS guarantee.

The wind-ripple follow-up replaces a sea-state-independent noise gradient with six directional normal waves of0.061–0.31m wavelength, below the FFT's0.45m minimum. Their frequency uses `omega²=9.81*k+0.000074*k³`, with a fixed approximate surface-tension/density coefficient. The BREEZE sum of virtual height amplitudes is2.538mm and peak slope bound0.084; these are normal-only detail, not additional geometric displacement or contact heights. Ocean strength scales their slopes; per-wave screen footprints fade periods from4 to2pixels and transfer unresolved energy to isotropic roughness. The footprint uses displaced world coordinates, matching the phase coordinates. Directional covariance, temperature/salinity variation and shallow-water dispersion remain approximations. Reflection mip levels now measure the final wave-distorted UV; hull fragments are discarded after that derivative calculation.

The coastal-cover follow-up uses [Poly Haven's Aerial Grass Rock](https://polyhaven.com/a/aerial_grass_rock), a photographed 15 m ground surface by Rob Tuytel, under [CC0](https://polyhaven.com/license). Its 2048×2048 diffuse, OpenGL normal and roughness maps are self-hosted with source MD5, local SHA-256 and byte counts recorded in the manifest. They blend with the existing 90 m rocky surface at their respective physical scales. The same height/slope cover weight controls all three channels; steep cliffs and the waterline retain the rocky material. Failed cover loading preserves the original rock path.

Tree roots and planting slopes use the terrain's rendered triangle interpolation. Dry ground below 35 degrees receives clustered sites; steep cliffs and low shoreline wash remain clear. Near trees retain the scanned geometry. Distant trees use eight yaw views baked from its photographed PBR materials, with diffuse colour normalized by sampled coverage after mip filtering to avoid mixing transparent black into foliage colour. The initial bake waits for the sky environment, and preset changes rebake using the same HDR environment and lights as the near trees. The atlas uses the renderer's sRGB encode/decode path, without an additional gamma transform or a second lighting pass. This remains a single-elevation impostor approximation; individual tree yaw, steep viewing angles, slow sky rotation and cast shadows are not reproduced exactly in its baked shading.

Propulsion foam follows the past horizontal stern positions of each analytical vessel route. Thirteen points at eight-second intervals cover up to 96 seconds; the last initial point is clamped to launch rather than evaluating a fictitious earlier voyage. Fragment shading takes the largest nearby segment contribution, so joins do not double the foam. Width grows with age and emission speed, intensity decays, and the oldest 16 seconds fade out. Expanded per-vessel bounds avoid evaluating all segments across the open sea. Short current-heading Kelvin ridges remain a local visual approximation, with an 80 m decay and a launch-time distance limit. This records route geometry, not fluid advection, interacting wakes or a hydrodynamic solution; pitch-induced horizontal stern shortening is omitted.

Paint maps use the green channel for roughness, which [Three.js multiplies by the material's roughness factor](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html#roughness). The passenger-vessel paint now uses factor 1 only after its photographed maps load; multiplying by its fallback 0.45 had reduced a sampled mean roughness of 0.284 to 0.128. Failed loading retains the 0.45 fallback. [UnrealBloomPass](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/UnrealBloomPass.html) thresholds linear HDR luminance before the final ACES output. Its strength/radius/threshold are now 0.035/0.35/2.4, reducing diffuse-paint halos while retaining bright-sky glare. [Filament's imaging guide](https://google.github.io/filament/main/filament.html#imagingpipeline) provides a physical-camera precedent; these fixed artistic settings are not a calibrated sensor, light meter or optical scattering model.

[Blue Metal Plate's photographed width is 2.5 m](https://polyhaven.com/a/blue_metal_plate). Painted structures now use ship-local metric UV charts: XZ on roofs, ZY on sides and XY on end-facing triangles. This replaces stretched Box UVs, 1 m Extrude UVs and differently scaled hull sides. Render-only chart splits retain every source triangle and normal; the closed source hull and contact geometry are preserved. UV charts are an axis projection approximation on curved surfaces and may have transitions at dominant-axis boundaries. Teak and label mappings keep their existing layout.

HIGH and ULTRA passenger cabin windows use [Three.js physical transmission](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html#transmission), with opacity1, transmission0.94, IOR1.5 and an approximate12mm volume thickness. Pale glazing avoids the previous dark diffuse tint suppressing transmitted room colour. Glazing casts no opaque depth shadow; walls and frames retain shadows. Actual6.22m-deep room geometry supplies floors, partitions, beds, seating and pleated curtains behind the panes. This is a synthetic cabin layout, not a surveyed ship or complete accommodation model. Three.js transmission samples opaque screen-space colour, so transparent guards and off-screen content remain approximation limits. The ocean reflector reuses the same camera's result until the next world update, avoiding duplicate reflection work between transmission and opaque passes while retaining full reflection resolution.

BALANCED uses alpha-transparent cabin panes at opacity0.18 with depth writes disabled. It keeps the same cabin geometry but approximates the optical glass without refraction. Changing to HIGH/ULTRA restores transmission0.94, opacity1 and depth writes. The Three.js r186 transmission pipeline adds opaque-background renders for both main and reflection cameras, with internal4×MSAA resolve and mipmap generation; composer.samples=0 does not disable those targets. A tested [transmissionResolutionScale](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGLRenderer.html#transmissionResolutionScale) of0.5 alone did not resolve software-GPU startup stalls, so it is not retained.

Cabin contact shading uses a ship-local visibility bake against copied opaque triangles. [Three.js aoMap](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html#aoMap) reads non-colour data through a separate UV set; the photographed paint and teak keep their original UVs. [three-mesh-bvh](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh) accelerates closest-hit queries within 1.2 m. Thirty-two cosine-weighted hemisphere rays estimate short-range visibility for floors, ceilings, rear walls, both partitions, facade reveals and balcony soffits. Glass is excluded from blockers. Separate 2 cm receiver panels expose the correct room-facing surfaces without changing drone contacts.

The 256×256 map shares one furnished cabin template across the rigid vessel; its opaque blockers include the neighbouring cabin and common slabs. Intensity0.75 attenuates indirect illumination instead of painting permanent black into the photographic diffuse maps. This is ambient visibility, not bounced-light transport or dynamic screen-space AO. Curtain variants, terminal cabins, chart filtering and furniture surfaces without receiver charts remain approximation limits. The bake currently runs once during loading, rather than every frame.
